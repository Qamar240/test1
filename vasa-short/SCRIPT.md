# The Vasa: the most powerful warship ever built (user script, 2026-11-06)
Facts (Vasa Museum / Wikipedia "Vasa (ship)"): ordered 1625 by King Gustav II Adolf, who kept changing specs while
campaigning in Poland; 64 bronze guns, hundreds of painted carvings; stability test by Admiral Klas Fleming: 30 men
ran across the deck three times, stopped because she nearly capsized; maiden voyage 10 Aug 1628 from Stockholm,
salute fired, capsized in a light gust after ~1,300 m, water entered through open lower gunports; ~30 died;
inquiry found nobody guilty; salvaged 1961 (333 years later), largely intact; Vasa Museum > 1 million visitors/yr.

Most powerful warship ever built. Sank 20 minutes into its very first voyage, right in front of the entire city.
In 1625, the king of Sweden ordered a ship that would rule the seas.
And while he was away at war, he kept changing the plans and pushing for it to be finished faster.
When it was done, the Vasa carried 64 bronze cannons and hundreds of painted carvings, and it was the pride of the whole Navy.
But before it sailed, the admiral ran a test.
He had 30 men run from one side of the deck to the other.
And after only three runs, the ship was rocking so badly that he stopped them because it was about to tip over in the harbor.
And still, nobody wanted to be the one to tell the king that his ship wasn't ready.
On August 10th, 1628, crowds lined the shore as the Vasa set sail and fired a salute from its cannons.
It had traveled barely a kilometer when a light gust of wind pushed it onto its side.
Water rushed in through the open gunports, and within minutes, it was sitting on the bottom of the ocean, taking around 30 people down with it.
At the trial, everyone blamed someone else, and in the end, nobody was punished.
But then, 333 years later, the ship was lifted out of the water almost perfectly intact.
And today, more than a million people a year come to see the most powerful warship ever built, which sank 20 minutes into its very first voyage.
